Historian's Handbook

For the one who tells the tale: running the table, and the Bestiary
Historian’s Eyes Only

Players, stop here. This book is for the Historian. It holds the numbers, tricks, and weaknesses of everything that hunts the wilds of Aiora, and knowing them ahead of time will spoil the fear of meeting them. If you’re a player, the Player’s Handbook has everything you need.

The Historian¶

In Agon, the game master is the Historian. You describe the world, voice everyone the players meet, set the Target Numbers for Trials, and run every monster. The players decide what their demi-gods and Aegis do; you decide how the world answers (see Premise of Play in the Player’s Handbook).

This book assumes you know the player rules. It adds what only the Historian needs: the rules that are yours to set, the didra market, and the Bestiary.

Part I Running the Game¶

Rules You Set¶

A few rules in the Player’s Handbook are left to you. Settle them before your first session and tell your players.

  • Long magic out of combat. Theion settles in about a minute of calm, so small magic outside combat is free. Decide whether rituals, long held spells, or magic under pressure (a chase, a collapsing tunnel) count against the Limit. See Theion.
  • The Didra Market. Track each city’s market, or leave every city Steady. See The Didra Market below.
  • Shooting past range. Decide whether attacks beyond a weapon’s range need a Trial. See Maximum Effective Range.
  • Resurrection timing. A dissipated demi-god can rise at once or later, if the player would rather play their Aegis for a while. See Health Points and Death.
  • Titles. You award titles and monikers when the party reaches milestones. See Reputation.

Rewards¶

Experience comes from felling enemies and completing challenges. The Exp for each monster is in its stat block and is split among the party; for monsters you build yourself, use the Exp rate under Building Your Own. Strong enemies and hard puzzles can give more, so that each level takes about as long as the last (see Leveling Up).

Monster Parts

Monster parts are reasons to hunt. A party that needs fire damage knows where chimeras nest, and a rumor of a Nemean lion in the hills is a quest all on its own. The parts and what they do are in Monster Parts.

Selling Craftwork

A town can only buy so much. A frontier settlement might take one rifle a month, while Pegemus will take a crate a week. Busy markets send crafters to the big cities, and the road there is where the stories are.

The Didra Market¶

Didra isn’t just money. It powers the lights, the pumps, and the relay towers that keep the ledger running. When a city runs short, its obol wobbles. This rule is optional: if you’d rather not track it, every city stays Steady.

Each city has a Market track that starts at Steady. Its multipliers stack on top of the city’s own rate and prices.

Market Didra pays Prices What it feels like
Glut ×0.6 ×0.9 Cheap power; clerks shrug at your crystals
Soft ×0.8 ×1.0 A good season; the refineries are full
Steady ×1.0 ×1.0 Normal
Tight ×1.3 ×1.1 Reserves run low; rates change daily
Crisis ×1.8 ×1.3 Blackouts, rationing, a run on the exchange

Moving the Market¶

  • Events. A refinery attacked or a fuel caravan lost moves a city 1–2 steps toward Crisis. A new didra seam, or a Legendary’s corpse hauled through the gates, moves it toward Glut.
  • Flooding. Selling more didra in one week than a city’s Depth pays the rest one step lower, and the Market slips one step toward Glut.
  • Settling. Each week, the Market moves one step back toward Steady unless something holds it in place. For surprises, roll 2d6 each week instead: on a 2 it moves a step toward Glut; on a 12, a step toward Crisis.

In a Crisis¶

  • Blackouts. Each day, roll 1d6. On a 1–2, the city’s ledger is offline: no obol changes hands, only barter and didra.
  • Rationing. Large purchases need a permit, and who you know matters.
  • A run on the exchange. Everyone wants crystals, not obol. The exchange markup can climb to 50% or more.
Using the Market

Shoot up a refinery and the city falls into Crisis: your didra is worth a fortune, but the city is dark, angry, and hunting you. Haul in a dragon’s hoard and the price crashes, so you’ll have to carry it elsewhere, with all the risk that brings.

Part II Bestiary¶

Everything that walks, flies, or slithers past the walls is written down here: mortal raiders and zealots, the masterless Frenzied that the Orphans of Hades left behind, the Ancients who are older than the Fall, and a few machines that never got the order to stop.

Monster Rules¶

Monsters follow the same rules as player characters unless their stat block says otherwise. Budgets assume one pair (a demi-god and their Aegis) for Minions, Standards, and Elites, two pairs for Legendary monsters, and three or more pairs for Mythic ones.

Hit rolls: d20 + the listed Hit bonus against the target’s Evasion, with Divine Strikes, Critical Hits, Grazes, and Misses as usual.

Turns: one Action, one Minor Action, Movement, and one Reaction per round.

Abilities: monsters don’t use Theion. An ability marked (Even rounds) can be used in rounds 2, 4, 6, and so on; one marked (Once) can be used once per fight, from round 2 on. In round 1, monsters simply attack. Any spell or Affinity a monster uses follows the same tags.

Traits: only the traits a monster has points in are listed; every other trait is 0. Curses against a monster check its Resistance (10 + trait points), as usual. The trait caps (4 per trait, 6 per attribute) are for characters, not monsters: a trait of 5 or 6 marks something beyond human scale, like a giant’s Strength or a dragon’s hide. Keep those for Ancients, Legendaries, and Mythics. At 6, Resistance is 16 and opposing it is a TN 22 Trial, so curses and contests against that trait are long shots even for a maxed-out character.

Elements: a monster with an element takes half damage from elements its own element is strong against, and +50% damage from elements strong against it. Shadow is the exception: its strength only applies to magic (shields, walls, spells), so a Shadow monster doesn’t halve other elements’ damage, and Shadow attacks deal no bonus damage to creatures. Light still deals +50% to Shadow monsters.

Frenzied: familiars with no will of their own. They’re immune to Charmed, Authority, and Charm, never flee, and leave didra when they die.

Legendary: a few monsters are too great to fall to a crowd. A Legendary monster takes two turns each round, at its Initiative and again at Initiative − 10, with a full set of actions on each turn and one Reaction per round. Each has a Breaking Point at half HP that changes how it fights, and it gets its (Once) abilities back when it reaches it. (Even rounds) abilities still work only once per round.

Mythic: follows every Legendary rule, but takes three turns each round (Initiative, −7, and −14). Built for three or four pairs; two pairs need strong allies, and one pair needs an army.

Seasoned: for every 3 levels the party is above a monster’s level, give it +1 Hit, +20% HP, and +1 damage on each attack.

Threat: add up the Threat of every monster in a fight (see the table). For one pair at the monsters’ level: Easy 2–3, Fair 4–5, Hard 6–7, Deadly 8+. A monster 2 or more levels above the party counts double.

Running Mortals

Mortals aren’t monsters: most of them want to live. When a group loses half its number or its leader, the rest flee or surrender. A captured raider can be questioned, bribed, or turned in.

Bounties

Every city pays for proof of a Fist of Tyrants captain, and Antillos pays for Orphan Blood clergy. The Fist pays too, for Brotherhood officers. Know who you’re selling to.

Undead and Spirits

Undead have bodies and can be cut, burned, and pinned. Spirits don’t: they’re Incorporeal unless their entry says otherwise, so bring elements, Divine Strikes, or a priest. Both hate Light.

Using Templates

A template sits on top of any stat block. Apply it, then adjust the monster’s Exp and Didra as the template says. Templates stack, but sparingly: an Orphan-Touched Revenant Minotaur is a fine boss for a mid-level pair, and a nightmare for anyone lower.

Running an Ancient

Ancients are older than the Orphans’ war, and most are guarding something: a hoard, a grove, a crossing. Many can be talked to, bargained with, or slipped past. A fight with one should be a choice the party makes, not an ambush.

Monster Parts

Ancients and the Restless are where the best Parts come from (see Monster Parts in the Player’s Handbook). Harvesting one takes a Creativity Trial (TN 12) before the body is looted or burned.
