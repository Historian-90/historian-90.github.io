Agon · Book I

World Lore

The history, powers, and peoples of Altea after Sword Fall

The Great Game¶

Agones (or agons) were contests or games for a prize during classical antiquity, Greece, but such games were not limited to feats of athleticism alone. Painters, poets, and bards could also compete against one another in an agon.

This agon is a roleplaying game, involving a group of players (minimum 3-4 recommended) and a game master who narrates and applies the rules of the game. The players control their own characters and explore the world created by the Historian, pitting wits and skills against their fellow man, the world, fantastical creatures, and cunning puzzles.

The basic rules of Agon make it an adaptable tabletop system, but it is primarily meant for the world of Altea, which incorporates Greek mythology into its own lore and existing systems. The Agon roleplaying system itself borrows ideas from several popular tabletop systems already, like Dungeons & Dragons 5e and FATE: Core System among others.

This book holds the world. The rules for playing in it are in the Player’s Handbook, and its monsters and secrets are in the Historian’s Handbook.

Part I The Fall¶

Altea. Once a vibrant, beautiful world…

Now reduced to ash and rubble. Where man once congregated, monsters consume wandering survivors. Nature reclaims the remnants of mighty metropolises, and the last bastions of humanity cower behind sacred walls.

The World of Man¶

Forty years have passed since the doomsday event known as Sword Fall. Since that time, humanity remains a shell of what it once was. If it hadn’t been for the insatiable thirst of the Orphans the world might still be a peaceful and whole place.

But unbridled power begets destruction…

Sixty years ago, twenty before Sword Fall, the gods that had always faithfully watched over Altea and walked among its denizens withdrew to the heavens. The half-human children of Hades had declared war against both their father and the rest of the pantheon. In open rebellion, the demi-gods abandoned all restraint and used their power to spread chaos, desperately inviting the intervention of the gods. Despite the atrocities they committed, the gods remained silent.

Two decades passed before the hour of judgment finally came. To this day, no one knows why the gods abandoned the world, or why the Orphans of Hades sought bloodshed and Olympus’ ire, but one thing was certain: the gods were done with the mortal realm. As their only and final act in the fruitless war, the gods petitioned Hephaestus to craft a Sword of Damocles for each of Hades’ children responsible for starting the war. Additional swords were crafted for each of the other demi-gods on Altea that aided their efforts. If they could not restrain their power, then they would die by it.

With Hermes’ speed, each sword was then swiftly and secretly placed over the heads of every rebel demi-god. Whenever they used their divine powers, no matter the reason, the sword would fall. And so they did. Every last one.

When the blades came down, a wake of absolute destruction followed. Cities were reduced to rubble, mountains cracked, and forests were vaporized in an instant. The gods had finally delivered their answer to the Orphans’ provocation.

As an ominous reminder, for any demi-gods that remained on the world below, no matter the light or darkness that dwelled in their hearts, a sword was fastened above them. Though the demi-gods were free to continue to use their parents’ power, they had to be mindful to never use enough to break the strings of their swords.

Part II The Current Age¶

Before Sword Fall, the world had progressed into an age of modernity and advanced industry. Armies needn’t be superior to one another in numbers as much as they needed to have the technological edge to win a fight. Information all around the world was available at everyone’s fingertips thanks to computer science. Disease and famine were the concerns of a distant past due to the leaping advancements in medicine and agriculture.

Now, however, the technologies and industry of man’s golden age are mostly lost, scattered over a broken and divided world. Though, fortunately, there is still enough to maintain a fair defense against the dark creatures that roam Altea. Most mortals reside behind impressive, encompassing walls that safeguard colonies of survivors. Within these walled citadels, new cities have begun to emerge. Some are beginning to rapidly ascend to the glistening urban centers that once dominated Altea, but they will never be able to grow beyond the constraints of their permanent borders… so long as monsters dwell without.

But the dark familiars that prey upon man are merely the external threat. Human nature, after all, did not cease with Sword Fall. Greed, contempt, and prejudice still stir in the hearts of mortals; and even in an age of apocalypse, they remain divided.

There are four great city-states that have managed to rise to a level of prominence in the new age. Each of them gives shelter to, and invigorates, a tribal mentality. Outside of the cities, there are also a number of groups, cults, and communities that struggle on in a dangerous world.

The Great City-States¶

Antillos¶

Antillosians primarily worship Zeus, Hera, and Athena. They are a republic led by a council of passionate statesmen that employ demi-gods to protect the city and its interests. While Olympian sons and daughters protect the city, demi-gods are actually not granted citizenship until they have proven their loyalty—which itself becomes a matter of debate on the senate floor.

Therone¶

A peaceful enclave of survivors, Theronians welcome outsiders without reservation. Their society is governed by a council of thirteen elected officials. Their defense is purchased rather than conscripted, and so the demi-gods that have pledged their loyalty to the city do so by accepting tribute. Their patron deities are Demeter and Dionysus, as well as, to a lesser extent, Hermes and Iris.

Tyríses¶

Seeing themselves as directly opposed to Antillos, but nonetheless hawkish, Tyrísians are a spartan society led by a king, supported by an oligarchic government. The highest ranking officers of their military are either demi-gods or priests that are well-learned in combat magics. As such, Tyrísian demi-gods are not only citizens but also of an elite class in the city-state’s social hierarchy. Patron deities of Tyríses include Ares, Kratos, and Nike.

Pegemus¶

An isolated city-state wholly uninterested in affairs beyond their walls, Pegemus strives to rebuild the world within its borders. They are arguably the most technologically advanced of the four great cities, having recovered much of what was lost all while continuously striving to achieve even greater heights. Pegemans do not idly rely on demi-gods for their protection. Instead, they employ technological defenses to ward off invading forces and pesky monsters. They do, however, employ demi-gods willing to aid in their research and industry. The chief patron deities of Pegemus are Hephaestus and Tyche.

No Man’s Land¶

Beyond the protection of the fortified cities there is only wilderness. Much of Altea was changed during Sword Fall, and even before the destructive power of the Damocles eruptions, the Orphans wiped out entire populations in their rampages. Old centers of civilization, abandoned to fall into disrepair, have crumbled. Nature has reclaimed what man once conquered and developed.

There are hardly any usable roads, few convenient bridges, or tunnels to pass through. Those that have managed to miraculously remain standing are on the brink of total collapse. The city-states, wanting to minimize risk, prefer not to contend with the threats that lie beyond the walls. You will find no engineers or utility workers outside of their safe havens.

Trade between the cities, and any settlements under their protection, is conducted by merchants that spare no expense on hiring the best mercenaries or security contractors. No small number of demi-gods can be found in this line of work.

Long-distance travel is always conducted by air when possible. However, since the collapse of wide-spread industry, reliable sources of fuel are difficult to come by and secure. Thus, only critical matters are privileged with air travel. Other transiting is conducted by land or water. When all-terrain vehicles reach a point of no-return or run into an obstacle that can’t be overcome, your only options are horseback (if available) or foot.

Lesser Factions¶

The Cult of Orphan Blood¶

The demi-gods that rebelled against Hades and the Olympians (known as the Orphans) may all be gone, slain by their Swords of Damocles, but they had amassed a following of fanatic mortals around the world. Many of them still worship their dead martyrs and hold fast to an unreasonable anger and hatred of the gods. They are well skilled in arcane magic, both primordial and conceptual. Even demi-gods have a difficult time contending with the cultists, and so it is ill-advised to face an enclave of them alone.

Anarchists¶

Less of a faction and more of a scattered people of kindred spirit, anarchists are mostly sole survivors or small groups of wanderers that want nothing to do with the city-states. Since the collapse of the old governments and wide-spread civilization, they have learned to live on their own and fend for themselves. Having grown accustomed to a nomadic lifestyle, they would rather die than surrender their near-perfect freedom. Most anarchists are traders, hunters, and gatherers. Few have much in the way of technical or magical skill, but having fought frenzied familiars and monsters for most of their lives, they are versed in conventional combat and know the way of the land better than most.

The Fist of Tyrants¶

For being gangs of outlaws, reprobates, murderers, and thieves, the five “fingers” of the Fist are surprisingly well-organized. They are tyrannical raiders that plague the no man’s land between the city-state territories. Where there aren’t any monsters there are sure to be scouts of the fingers nearby… waiting to ambush your camp at night. The gangs are notorious for pillaging easy settlements, taking slaves, and murdering any that try to stop them. One full gang is considered a single finger of the overall fist. Each is led by something of a captain, but it is not known who (or, indeed, if anyone) commands the entire Fist. Each of the city-states offer varied bounties for slain gang members, provided there is proof of the kill.

Monsters and the Frenzied¶

In the wilds, expect any number of threats and bad luck. Foul weather is the least of your concerns when you’re treading through territory overrun by frenzied familiars. And those aren’t the only monsters you need to be wary of. During their spree of terror, the Orphans unleashed all kinds of beasts on the world: minotaurs, gorgons, sirens, and cyclops… to name the more common ones.

“The Frenzied” is the pejorative, all-encompassing term used to refer to the familiars created by the Orphans of Hades during their rebellion. This name was given due to the very fact that, without their masters, they are aimless predators that know no reason, only operating on basic instinct driven by a lust for mortal flesh. While it is normal for a familiar to “frenzy” when the covenant with their master is severed, it is not right that they would yearn for blood like monsters. It is hypothesized that the Orphans encoded this as a condition when they were created.

Most of the Frenzied are all the same in both appearance and function: four-legged animals that look like a hybrid between a wolf and a panther. These types of familiars are called cerebos. They are completely black, save for the purple glow of their eyes and the flicker of an electric field around their body when channeling magic. Furthermore, their bodies absorb and trap light rather than reflect it, making them nigh impossible to spot in the dark until they use their affinities.

Commerce¶

All of the city-states use their own contained digital blockchain currency, something salvaged from the previous age. The universal name for all digital currencies is simply “obol”. However, as there is no longer a worldwide web of information that can unite these currencies and form a single network, they are isolated, remaining entirely separate from one another. Each individual that has registered with the city’s government is granted an account that tracks their amount of obol for that city-state.

New obol is generated into the blockchain by collecting an alternative energy source from slain monsters and familiars. This energy is called didra. It crystallizes as a jagged purple mineral when expelled from the dispersing corpse of a creature that was manifested by a covenant or summoned by dark magic. The natural-born offspring of dark monsters (such as minotaurs, sirens, etc.) can also produce didra upon death. How much attained correlates to the creature’s overall strength.

Due to the sheer danger involved in collecting didra, it is marked as a valuable and precious resource, making it the perfect substance upon which to back a currency. It is primarily used to power both common electronics as well as complex machinery and can be converted into a slow-burning liquid fuel once purified (that is infinitely more efficient than petrol).

Didra can be turned over to exchange centers for obol. Each city-state, because their blockchains do not operate on an international network, has adopted its own exchange rates for didra. These rates will vary based on supply and demand; a fundamental truth in all economies. Because didra also keeps the lights on, a city’s rate can swing hard when its power runs short. See Equipment & Economy in the Player’s Handbook for prices and rates.

Settlements outside of a city-state’s broadcast network will not be able to use the blockchain. Thus, obol is not accepted as a form of payment for services and products. In these remote locations you’ll most likely encounter a this-for-that system of commerce; so be sure to carry a few commodities or valuable tools meant specifically for trading whenever you venture out.

Communication¶

Every established city-state has a zone of influence. Within this area, you’ll enjoy the convenience of clear radio transmissions and data feeds to phones. But this commodity only comes by way of working relay towers. This is the primary purpose of a city wanting to protect the settlements beyond their walls, as these are where you’ll find those towers. A settlement that has a relay tower is connected to a city’s obol blockchain, so you can also expect to find an exchange center there.

But settlements are prone to being overrun by monsters or bandits. If you find that you’ve suddenly lost tower signal when you should otherwise have it, then the nearest settlement might be in trouble.

Part III Divine Inheritance¶

The Twin-Soul Covenant¶

The Aegis¶

Since the gods in Olympus still refused to return to Altea, their remaining children below would need to take their place as protectors. However, with their divine power tempered by the looming Swords of Damocles, they had to find another means by which to fight.

During the chaos, the world became a haven for wild beasts and monsters that preyed upon mortal man; abominations brought forth by the Orphans in their petty rebellion. Most of these twisted creatures were beastly familiars, created by the dark magic of the Orphans. Without their masters, they are mindless and feral. Nonetheless, their frenzied nature proved a difficult challenge to the chained demi-gods. Though immortal, cursed to resurrect should they be stricken by anything but a true god’s power, many of them fell again and again to the savage familiars. It was a relentless cycle of painful death.

Athena, having briefly stolen to Altea to aid the children of Olympus, offered her wisdom. If the sons and daughters of the Olympians fought fire with fire, they would have a solid chance at protecting the world. She taught them a method where they could use just enough of their divine gifts to forge for themselves familiars of their own; one each.

When Athena instructed the Olympian children in the way of summoning a familiar, she revealed a secret: that a familiar’s form need not be one thing. After all, the familiars needed to be better and smarter than the lowly yet crafty beasts they would fight. At the same time, they would need to be durable, quick, and capable of generating their own power… the perfect weapons.

Thus, the demi-gods gave form and thought to familiars that could take two shapes: man and weapon. Named for their duty to both their masters and Altea—and in tribute to Athena—they are the Aegis . Bound by a covenant, they heed their master’s words and obey their every command. When called to fight, they shape into a weapon of their master’s own design. As long as the Aegis knows and has learned the fundamental construct of a weapon, they can shapeshift into it.

As their spirits come from Gaea, from her they draw their strength and power. As the mother of all creation, Gaea grants the Aegis the ability to choose their own Affinity. Most choose to complement their master’s gifts, bolstering their raw power with the same or similar affinities, or cover for weaknesses with opposing attributes.

Binding with a Familiar

Familiars are the bonding of three essential elements: flesh, mind, and the creator’s will.

  • First, the flesh is offered and made into a vessel by way of sacrifice. That of a human is taboo and would result in the creation of a kakós daemon (an evil demon), rather than a familiar. Thus, a beast of the world should serve as the vessel. However, as the original flesh would likely be either too weak or too limited for its purpose, it is reforged into the shape of a human while retaining some distinct animalistic traits.

  • Second, the mind is the familiar’s spirit, given by Gaea. It is unique, whole, and eager to live. It determines the familiar’s personality, desires, and passions.

  • Third, the creator’s will is the anchor that holds the familiar to the master. Conceptualized as the covenant, so long as the creator holds his will as absolute, the familiar will not frenzy or betray their master. The loss of the master’s will results in the familiar becoming like the monsters that roam the world.

Death and Resurrection¶

Being partially mortal, a demi-god can still know death, but only temporarily at the hands of man. The slumber of true death can only come by the judgment and power of an Olympian (a true god). If killed by any other means, a demi-god will dissipate and later rise again at an altar to their parent. Additionally, as long as their covenant with their Aegis is still intact, the familiar will enjoy the same benefits of immortality.

Most temples to the gods outside of the walled cities have long since been destroyed; their altars were corrupted by the taint of monsters or abandoned altogether. Therefore, any likely place of resurrection will be within the nearest protected city or settlement. So long as a priest continues to bless an altar, a child of its patron god may awaken there.

The rules for dissipating and rising again are in Health Points and Death.

Theion¶

Magical creatures, including familiars and demi-gods, weave powerful goetia and mageia using Theion: a divine energy that permeates Altea, and doubles not only as the impetus for Affinities, but also the veil that shrouds the universe’s greatest secrets.

The four colors of Theion have names of their own. Red Theion is called Photia, yellow is Líthos, green is Aéras, and blue is Húdōr. Though they should not be correlated with the elements, they were named for Fire, Stone, Air, and Water, respectively, because of their vibrant colors when manifested as energy particles during experiments conducted by Pegemus scientists and a need to name the discoveries.

An actual clock-like device can be carried on a person to measure their accumulation of Theion energies. This is the Theion Gauge in-world.

How the gauge works at the table is in Theion & the Limits of Magic.

The Gods¶

Each god of the pantheon holds dominion over at least one primordial element and a handful of conceptual truths, and passes that dominion to their children. The full list of gods and the Affinities they grant is in The Gods & Affinities. The patrons of each city-state are listed under The Great City-States.
