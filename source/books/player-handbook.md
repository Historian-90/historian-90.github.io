## The Great Game {#the-great-game}
Agones (or agons) were contests or games for a prize during classical antiquity, Greece, but such games were not limited to feats of athleticism alone. Painters, poets, and bards could also compete against one another in an agon.

This agon is a roleplaying game, involving a group of players (minimum 3-4 recommended) and a game master who narrates and applies the rules of the game. The players control their own characters and explore the world created by the Historian, pitting wits and skills against their fellow man, the world, fantastical creatures, and cunning puzzles.

The basic rules of Agon make it an adaptable tabletop system, but it is primarily meant for the world of Altea, which incorporates Greek mythology into its own lore and existing systems. The Agon roleplaying system itself borrows ideas from several popular tabletop systems already, like *Dungeons & Dragons 5e* and *FATE: Core System* among others.

### The Three Books
*Agon* is split into three books:

* The **[World Lore](@lore)** tells the history of Altea: Sword Fall, the four great city-states, the factions of the wilds, and the covenant between a demi-god and their Aegis. Read it first.
* This **Player's Handbook** teaches you to play. It's split into four parts and a set of appendices:
    * **Part I:** The **Core Rules** teach you how to play the game at a basic level, getting you ready to roll the dice.
    * **Part II:** In **Character Creation**, you'll put together both your demi-god and your Aegis.
    * **Part III:** **Divine Power** breaks down the magic system (Goetia and Mageia) and every Affinity.
    * **Part IV:** **Equipment & Economy** covers money and what it buys: obol and didra, everyday goods, weapons, armor, and crafting.
    * **Appendices:** the gods and their Affinities, the full Spell List, Boons and Curses, and Items.
* The **[Historian's Handbook](@historian)** is for the game master: running the table, the didra market, and the Bestiary. Players should leave it closed.

# <span class="num">Part I</span> Core Rules {#core-rules}

## Foundational Rules
### Premise of Play
Like most tabletop roleplaying games, *Agon*, at its core, is a story-driven collaboration between the players and Historian; where the Historian presents a challenge or fork in the narrative that allows the players to respond in a manner they deem fit to advance the plot or action.

An example round of play in Agon would go like this:

* The Historian first describes the scene, and in so doing directs the players' attention to a non-player character (NPC) that is brandishing a sword in the party's direction and shouting threats.
* The players choose to respond to the threats with diplomacy, rather than force, and talk their way out of the situation. They state their intentions to the Historian and explain what they wish to do—or they roleplay out their dialogue with the NPC, with the Historian assuming the voice of their harasser.
* The Historian may decide to have the players make a roll of the dice to see if their decision to charm the NPC is successful.
* Let's say the roll is successful. The Historian may then narrate how the NPC changes their tone, becoming less threatening and more open to dialogue.
* But if the roll failed... The Historian chooses to then have the party roll for initiative, because the NPC *charges at the party*, beginning a round of combat.

This loop plays over throughout the game, whether in combat or not. While specific rulesets change depending on the situation, the overall cycle remains: the Historian narrates and presents a dilemma → the players respond to the dilemma →  the Historian narrates the consequences.

So on and so forth.

The next few sections will go over *how* the players respond to situations that call for breaking out the dice.

### The Big 3: Narrative, Action, Trial
There are three (3) kinds of things your character does in *Agon*: **Narrative** moves, **Actions**, and **Trials**.

**Narrative** moves are things done by a character outside (but sometimes inside) combat that serve to position the character, change or advance the scene, or interact with an object or person for purposes of roleplay or story development. Most narrative moves are free to execute at will when outside of combat encounters. While inside combat encounters, narrative moves may be performed at the discretion of the Historian and may cost a turn.

**Actions** are what you do on your turn in combat: attacking and defending against opponents, repositioning to a new point entirely, grappling, dodging, assisting, recovering, and using active magic. Your character may perform one of these actions per turn. See [Ready, Aim, Fire!](#ready-aim-fire) for reloading. Alongside their one Action, your character may also take one **Minor Action** per turn (see *Minor Actions*).

:::sidebar
##### Movement in Combat
Movement during combat (running, sprinting, walking, strafing, crawling, etc.) can be done **once per turn** without consuming an action, so long as the distance traveled does not exceed your character's **Speed**.
:::

Finally, **Trials** require a **1d20** roll to determine success and include responses to challenges, rolled against a Target Number set by the Historian or triggered traps.

![Two warriors play at a game board with a twenty-sided die](art/game.svg)

### Roll Outcomes for Trials
Every roll in *Agon* works the same way: roll a **1d20**, add your modifier, and compare the total to a **Target Number (TN)**. Meet or beat it and you succeed; how far above or below it you land decides the rest. For Trials, the Historian sets the TN by how hard the task is. Most everyday tasks shouldn't need a roll at all. When you work against another character (charming, deceiving, grappling), the TN is **10 + their trait modifier**.

| Easy | Standard | Hard | Very Hard | Heroic | Mythic |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 28 |

##### Trials use a 1d20
| Roll               |                  | Outcome          |
|:------------------:|:-----------------|:-----------------|
| Natural 20         | **Critical Success** | You succeed with style and are granted an **agathós daemon**: a good spirit you may call upon to guarantee one success when desired. |
| TN or higher       | **Success** | You pass the challenge. |
| 1-4 below TN       | **Close Call** | You succeed, just barely. No cost; the Historian simply describes how close it was. |
| 5+ below TN        | **Failure** | You fail, and nothing worse. A natural 1 is just a 1: your modifiers still count. |

### Turn Order and Rounds in Combat
Which character goes first in a combat encounter is decided by **Initiative**: roll a **1d20** and add your **Agility** modifier, plus anything else that affects it. Only roll Initiative when the Historian says so; it's the official start of a combat encounter.

Whether your character deals damage is decided by a **Hit** roll: **1d20** plus your **Strength** points for melee attacks or your **Perception** points for ranged attacks (some weapons use **Agility**; see [Weapons](#weapons)), against the target's **Evasion**. When everyone has taken a turn, the **Round** ends, Theion bands advance by 1, and a new round begins.

##### Hit rolls use a 1d20
| Roll               |                  | Outcome          |
|:------------------:|:-----------------|:-----------------|
| Natural 20         | **Divine Strike** | It always hits: the maximum on every damage die, doubled (bonuses aren't doubled). |
| Crit range, and it hits | **Critical Hit** | Roll double the damage dice. |
| Evasion or higher  | **Hit** | Roll your damage. |
| 1-4 below Evasion  | **Graze** | Deal only your damage bonus (minimum 1). Curses don't apply. |
| 5+ below Evasion   | **Miss** | No damage. A natural 1 is just a 1: your modifiers still count. |

:::note
##### Order of Play in Combat
* The Historian says "roll for initiative."
* Everyone rolls a **1d20** + their **Agility** modifier; the Historian rolls for each NPC. Highest goes first.
* On your turn, take your Action, Minor Action, and Movement in any order.
* To attack, roll for Hit against the target's **Evasion**. The target may spend its Reaction to **Dodge** or **Parry**.
* On a Hit, roll damage (double the dice on a Critical Hit). On a Graze, deal your damage bonus.
* Resolve for damage.
:::

### Critical Hits
Every weapon has a **Crit** number in its table. A *natural* Hit roll at or above that number that also hits is a **Critical Hit**: roll **double the damage dice** (bonuses aren't doubled). Affinities like *Archery* and *Martial Prowess* can widen the range.

A **natural 20** always hits, and it's the best result in the game: a **Divine Strike**, dealing the maximum on every damage die, doubled (again, bonuses aren't doubled). A Critical Hit can at best tie it.

:::sidebar
*Keen* weapons widen the crit range by 2. *Brutal* weapons triple the dice on a Critical Hit or Divine Strike instead of doubling them.
:::

### Reactions
#### In Combat
Each character gets **one Reaction per round**, which refreshes at the start of their turn. After an attack roll against you is made, you may spend your Reaction to raise your **Evasion** against that one attack:

* **Dodge:** + your **Agility** points (minimum +1).
* **Parry:** + your **Strength** points (minimum +1), plus the **Guard** of a shield you hold. Needs a melee weapon or shield in hand.
* **Riposte:** if your Parry turns a hit into a Miss, make one melee attack right away.

This can turn a Hit into a Graze, or a Graze into a Miss. A Divine Strike can't be dodged or parried.

When an enemy in melee range tries to move away from you, you may spend your Reaction to make one attack against it (unless you're Bound, Prone, or Slowed). Some Affinities, like *Protection*, also use your Reaction.

#### Out of Combat
When a trap springs or the ground gives way, the Historian calls for a **Trial** to react, usually with **Agility**, though **Perception** or **Strength** might save you instead. The TN depends on the danger.

#### Takedowns
Against a target that is **unaware of you** (you're unseen and it hasn't noticed you), within melee range and armed with a melee weapon or bare hands, you can spend your Action on a **Takedown**: an **Agility** or **Strength** Trial (TN 12) instead of a Hit roll. You choose kill or knockout.
* **Minion.** *Success:* it drops, silently. *Close Call:* it drops, but makes a noise. *Failure:* a normal hit, and it's aware of you.
* **Standard.** *Success:* it drops, silently. *Close Call:* it drops to 1 HP and is **Stunned**, unable to call out. *Failure:* a normal hit, and it's aware.
* **Elite or Legendary.** Can't be taken down. *Success:* a Critical Hit. *Close Call:* a normal hit. *Failure:* a normal hit, and it's aware.

### Weal and Woe
When your character has a logical **advantage** on a roll, the Historian may grant **Weal**: add **1d4** to the total. When they're logically at a **disadvantage**, they roll with **Woe**: subtract **1d4**. Weal and Woe cancel each other out.

### Aegis in Combat
#### Covenant Arms
An Aegis can fight beside its master in human form with its own gear, weapons, and magic, or it can become the weapon in its master's hand. A wielded Aegis never acts on its own: it doesn't cast, and it doesn't move your arm. But it sees, it burns with its own element, and it shares your immortality. While you wield your Aegis:

* **Guided Hand.** Through the covenant, your Aegis whispers what it sees: *there, now, duck.* Add **+1d4** to Hit rolls made with its form, and to your **Dodge** and **Parry**. Other weapons don't get the bonus.
* **Watchful.** Two minds watch the field. You get a **second Reaction** each round, but never two against the same attack.
* **Living Element.** The weapon is your Aegis's body, alive with its essence. On a Hit or better, add the **Aegis's Power points** as damage of one of its primordial elements, chosen when it transforms. A form that attacks only **once** per Action adds it **twice**. Elemental strengths apply. This bonus isn't doubled by Critical Hits.
* **The Covenant Holds.** When a hit would drop you to 0 HP, your Aegis may take the rest onto its own HP, leaving you at 1. If that drops the Aegis to 0, it dissipates.

Fighting side by side ends fights faster and holds two positions. Wielding your Aegis is slower, but much harder to break.

#### Transformation Rules
Transforming mid-combat is possible at any time and does not require any rolls to determine success. It costs one **Minor Action**, and who pays depends on who decides:

* **On the master's command,** it costs the master's Minor Action.
* **On the Aegis's own initiative,** it costs the Aegis's Minor Action. An Aegis in weapon form has no actions of its own, so choosing to transform back into human form starts its turn early, and the Aegis spends that turn's Minor Action to do it.

If the master dissipates or is otherwise not able to summon their Aegis, then the Aegis must act on its own initiative.

**Human to Weapon.** The Aegis must be within **10 meters** of its master. It dissolves into its weapon or shield form and flies straight into its master's hand.

**Weapon to Human.** The Aegis takes form in a space beside its master and joins the Turn Order (see *Turn Order* below).

**Weapon to Weapon.** Changing an Aegis from one learned weapon form to another counts as a **Swap** and costs the master's Minor Action. While it remains a weapon, an Aegis is its master's tool and has no actions of its own.

When transformed, an Aegis' may still benefit from passive Affinities that do not logically require them to be in their human form. They cannot cast active magic, use active Affinities, or take independent movements.

#### Turn Order
When transformed into a weapon or shield, an Aegis shares their master's placement in the Turn Order.

An Aegis that begins an encounter in human form rolls for Initiative as normal.

When an Aegis returns to human form during combat, it does **not** roll for Initiative. It takes the place directly after its master in the Turn Order and **acts immediately**, taking its Action, its Movement, and its Minor Action (unless it spent that Minor Action on the transformation). It keeps this place for the rest of the encounter.

**One Turn Per Round.** An Aegis can take only one turn per round. If it has already acted this round, it can't transform on its own initiative. Its master can still command it back into human form, and it takes its place behind its master, but it can't act until the next round.

#### HP as a Weapon
An Aegis's HP stays the same whether it's in weapon or human form.

Successful attacks directly against them, even in their weapon form, impact your Aegis' HP. As a weapon or shield, they receive <u>no benefits from Armor</u>, but they can still receive benefits from **Attributes**, **Affinities**, and active magical effects like **boons**.

### Minor Actions
Once per turn, alongside their Action and Movement, a character may take one **Minor Action**. Minor Actions are quick moves that set up or support an action. They never deal damage on their own. A Minor Action can be used to:

* **Transform an Aegis** between human and weapon form (see *Aegis in Combat*).
* **Reload** a ranged weapon.
* **Swap** one weapon or held item for another, including changing an Aegis from one weapon form to another.
* **Hold a Defensive Concentration spell**, or cast one whose description allows it, such as *Aspis* (see *Concentration*).
* **Use a consumable** item on yourself or an adjacent ally (a medical kit heals **1d6 + 2**).

Simply drawing or stowing a weapon is free to narrate and doesn't need a Minor Action. Anything else requires the Historian's approval. An unused Minor Action is lost at the end of your turn and can't be traded for a second Action.

### Concentration
Some spells have a Duration of **Concentration**, meaning the caster keeps the spell going from turn to turn. Every Concentration spell is either **Offensive** (it deals damage, like *Potamos*) or **Defensive** (it protects, like *Aspis*).

* **Casting** a Concentration spell costs your Action, unless the spell says otherwise.
* **Holding** it on each of your later turns costs your **Action** for an Offensive spell, or your **Minor Action** for a Defensive spell. If you don't pay, the spell ends.
* While holding a spell, one hand is busy with it and you move at **half Speed**. This doesn't stack: holding two spells still leaves you at half Speed.
* The spell also ends if you're knocked prone or forcibly moved, or if you take damage and fail an **Endurance** Trial. Damage absorbed by a shield doesn't count.
* The Theion band used for the spell doesn't reset until the spell ends.
* You can hold more than one Concentration spell at once, but each must draw on a **different Theion band**. A band's energy can only feed one spell at a time, so two Blue band spells (like *Aspis* and *Potamos*) can't be held together.

### Evasion
**Evasion** is the number an attack roll must meet or beat to hit you, the way a target's defenses are measured in *Agon*.

**Evasion = 10 + Agility points − armor penalty** (Light 0, Medium −1, Heavy −2). A held shield with **Guard** adds to it. Creatures list their Evasion in their stat block.

Bonuses to Hit rolls, like an Aegis's **Guided Hand**, add to the total before it's compared to Evasion, and a Reaction's **Dodge** or **Parry** adds to Evasion after the roll.

## Adventuring
### Resting
Your demi-god may have divine blood and magic flowing through them, but they are not robots. Every so often, you will need to let them take a break and rest. A **Full Rest** allows for the recovery of all **Hit Points (HP)** lost [see note below], allows your Aegis to progress in **Learning** new weapon designs, gives you time to **Craft**, and also allows you to **Level Up** and assign **Trait points**.

<u>A Full Rest is approximately half a day</u>. Anything less is considered a **Short Rest**, and only allows you to recover half of your HP lost. Characters will not have time to do anything else, save for light roleplaying.

:::note
##### HP Recovery While Resting
Recovering HP on a rest takes a **Medical Kit**: 1 kit per character, per rest. Without one, you regain only what magic heals during the rest: a healing spell (*Iasis*, *Pēgē*) or an ally's *Vitality* blood heals its normal amount. The *Vitality* Affinity heals its owner to full on any rest.
:::
:::note
##### Rough Camping
In harsh weather (driving rain, snow, bitter cold), a Full Rest needs shelter: a tent, or at least blankets. Without it, the rest counts only as a Short Rest.
:::

### Learning

Aegis must learn the weapons their demi-gods want them to shapeshift into, and learning something always takes time—time that you'll  need to set aside during your adventures.

Each weapon type has a **Learning Time (LT)** noted next to it in the **Weapon Tables**. <u>LT is always measured in Full Rests</u>. Remember: your Aegis can only devote themselves to learning a weapon when they have the time to spend on the task. So, for a weapon that requires 1 LT to learn, you need to take at least 1 Full Rest.

Does this mean they need to spend *every* minute of that rest with their nose in a schematic? Realistically, no. Practicing the shape shifting, understanding techniques for using the weapon, meditating on its design... these are some examples of good roleplaying to help flesh out that "learning process".

Don't think you need to rush through the LT requirement. In fact, you're *meant* to take your time here. If you take enough Full Rests throughout your campaign, your Aegis will be a master shapeshifter in no time, with a whole arsenal at their disposal.

### Crafting {#crafting-on-a-rest}

While your Aegis is learning new weapons, your demi-god can craft them! Crafting weapons and armor can be the most efficient way of staying armed and protected in the apocalyptic wild.

During a Full Rest period, you can use materials you've gathered during your adventures to create new weapons and armor. Crafting new items requires the correct schematic in addition to the raw materials, but characters with the Craftsmanship Affinity can throw out the manual (no need for dusty schematics when you have god-given talent).

You can read more about crafting in [Materials & Crafting](#materials-crafting).

### Reputation

As your characters progress through the story, complete quests, and help (or burden) others, their deeds will send ripples to all four city-states and beyond. A notable reputation can give access to opportunities not otherwise available to you, but a disreputable infamy can also see you barred from settlements, tracked by bounty hunters, or welcomed by other outcasts. "Good" and "evil" are not defined by you, but your *deeds* and the people they affect.

Your characters may also be awarded monickers or titles that people will come to know them by. These are given by the Historian, and are based on a combination of your deeds, style, and/or looks when you've reached certain milestones in your adventures.

# <span class="num">Part II</span> Character Creation {#character-creation}

## From Humble, *Divine* Beginnings...

### The Character Sheet
You will be creating two characters for this game. One is your demi-god, and the other is your demi-god's **Aegis** (their companion familiar that can shapeshift into weapons). If you're used to playing tabletop games where you only have to manage *one* character, don't worry—your demi-god is your "main" character. The Aegis is more like a *sidekick*, but you will still need a character sheet for them, as they'll have their own levels, inventory, traits, and affinities. With this in mind, the character sheet is designed to be as simplistic as possible, so players aren't having to juggle too much information at once.

The interactive character sheet works in any browser and inside the Owlbear Rodeo table; it does the math for you as you fill it in.
#### Name
Choose a good name for your character. Since the lore for *Agon* games pulls heavily from Greek mythology, try coming up with a Hellenic name.

*Examples include: Thyreos, Ktesias, Agamedes, Doros, Kyrene, Aglaia, Aletheia, and Medea.*

#### Child of...
If your sheet is for a demi-god, see [The Gods & Affinities](#the-gods-affinities) for a list of the gods. You may choose ONE god to be your divine parent.

For Aegis, this will be the god they have chosen to worship.

#### Favored by...
This is optional and largely depends on how you want to pick your Affinities (see [Affinities](#affinities-on-the-sheet) below). The god that favors you cannot be your divine parent (or the god you worship, if Aegis), but they will give you one of their own Affinities in exchange for giving up one of your parent's (or patron's).

#### Primary Faction...
You may start already allied to or a member of a faction. This can be one of the four city-states, or one of the lesser factions (see [The Great City-States](@lore#the-great-city-states) and [Lesser Factions](@lore#lesser-factions) in the *World Lore*).

#### Obol Accounts...
This is where you record your obol (see [Obol & Didra](#obol-didra)). Your **demi-god's Starter Kit** gives you a purse of obol to begin with; split it among the four accounts however you like. <u>Your demi-god and Aegis share the same accounts</u>, so if you record your obol on both character sheets, keep the amounts matching. Or record it on one sheet and leave the other blank.

#### Didra...
You start with **no didra**. Unlike obol, didra are physical crystals, so a demi-god and their Aegis <u>don't</u> share them: whoever picks them up carries them.

#### Title...
Leave this blank until the Historian awards you a title.

:::note
##### Starting Out
1. Your demi-god and your Aegis each choose a **Starter Kit** (see *Inventory*).
2. Your demi-god's kit gives you your starting **obol**. The Aegis's kit has no purse.
3. Your weapons, gear, and ammo come from your kits, and your Aegis starts knowing the **Aegis form** from its kit.
4. You start with no didra. Go earn some.
:::

#### Exp...

Leave this field blank for now until the Historian awards you Experience Points (Exp) in the game.

#### Affinities... {#affinities-on-the-sheet}

Both your demi-god and Aegis are allowed 4 Affinities, each. **For your demi-god**, either all 4 will come from your divine parent, or 3 will come from your parent and 1 from the god that favors them.

This rule is similar **for your Aegis**: they will either take 4 from the god they worship, or 3 from their patron and 1 from the god that favors them. An Affinity that grants a permanent trait point still obeys the trait caps (see *The Attribute Triangle*). If the point won't fit, put it in another trait instead.

#### Weapons... {#weapons-on-the-sheet}

See the [weapon tables](#weapons) for the weapons you can list here.

Remember, your Aegis can shapeshift into weapons themselves, but when not shifted, your demi-god will want to have a dedicated backup or two, and your Aegis will want to have weapons of their own when in human form and separated from their master.

Your starting weapons come from your **Starter Kit**.

##### Aegis Forms...

The weapons your Aegis can become are kept apart from the weapons you carry. On the **Aegis's sheet**, mark each form *learned* or *still learning*; only learned forms can be taken. At the start of the game, your Aegis knows the **Aegis form** from its Starter Kit. List the same forms on the demi-god's sheet to track their ammunition.

##### Ammunition...

Ranged weapons require ammunition. Record their ammunition count here, with *loaded* on the left, and *stock* on the right.

Your starting ammunition is listed in your **Starter Kit**.

:::note
##### Signature Trait
When **Strength**, **Agility**, **Perception**, or **Power** reaches **4**, you may name it your **Signature**. At levels **5**, **10**, and **15**, it adds **+1** to Hit rolls and **+1** to damage with the weapons or spells that use it (+3 each by level 15). You have one Signature, and you can only change it when you level up. An Aegis's Power Signature also adds to its *Living Element* damage while it's wielded.
:::

### The Attribute Triangle

The **Attribute Triangle** is used to visually assign and denote the spread of your character’s trait points. The **Body**, **Soul**, and **Mind** attributes each hold up to **6** trait points, and no single trait can hold more than **4**. In total, by your character’s max level, your triangle will have gained a total of 18 points.

:::note
##### Example:
For the Body attribute: You can assign 4 trait points to Strength and 1 point each to Agility and Endurance. This will suffice for a 6-point spread in the Body attribute.
:::

**At the start of the game**, you will have **4** trait points you may assign freely. You can do this by filling in or marking a circle for that trait in the triangle.

For Trials where you can modify the result of a roll using an appropriate Trait, you multiply the value of your Trait score (the number of points assigned to it) by 2 and the result is your modifier.

**Points or modifier?** Rolling against a creature (a Hit roll against Evasion, a curse against Resistance)? Add your **points**. Rolling against the world (a Trial against a TN)? Add your **modifier**.

:::note
##### Example:
If your character is relying on their Agility to leap over a gap while escaping a rolling boulder, and they have 2 points invested in Agility, they can multiply that score by 2 to get a modifier of +4 that they can add to their Trial when making the leap.
:::

![The Attribute Triangle](art/attribute-triangle.svg)

#### Agility
Agility determines your character’s ability to dodge certain attacks from enemies, save themselves from unexpected traps, vault obstacles, and sneak when stealth is called for. More fundamentally, it also determines their **Speed** (see [Speed](#speed)), and replaces Strength for Hit rolls with *Light* and *Finesse* weapons.
#### Strength
Strength determines your character’s overall physical prowess, their ability to lift heavy objects, and grapple or hold others. Each point adds +1 to **melee Hit** and melee/thrown **damage** rolls (twice that with *Heavy* weapons).
#### Endurance
Endurance determines your character’s overall stamina and constitution, and directly influences their total **HP**. For every trait point assigned to Endurance, you will gain +2 HP.
#### Reasoning
Reasoning determines your character’s ability to deduce conclusions from information presented to them. High reasoning ability may allow you to bypass some **puzzles** in the world, or at least seek a hint from the Historian.
#### Wit
Wit determines your character’s **charisma**, quick-thinking skills, ability to charm others and resist *being* **charmed** (see [Boons and Curses](#boons-and-curses)).

#### Perception
Perception determines your character’s **awareness** of events, people, objects, and anything concealed or hidden (some of these things require a minimum amount of points invested in Perception to identify). Each point adds +1 to **ranged Hit** rolls, and half that (rounded down) to ranged damage; *Deadeye* weapons add all of it.
#### Intuition
Intuition determines your character’s ability to draw conclusions from the imperceptible. It governs a **sixth sense** that can hint at another’s intentions, guide you to discovering a secret, or help you tell when someone is lying. High intuition can also grant free **Reactions** to certain attacks and challenges. Each point adds +1 to **Hit** rolls with spells.
#### Power
Power determines your character’s mastery of the arcane. It directly modifies the potency of their **Primordial spells** (**damage** and **healing** dealt).
#### Creativity
Creativity determines your character’s ability to think outside the box. It primarily influences **crafting**: crafting Trials add your Creativity modifier, and every point takes **5% off** the materials a craft needs, up to **50% off** (see [Crafting](#crafting-rules)).

### Leveling Up
Player characters and non-player characters alike have quantified levels of power. At the start of a standard game, **all player characters begin at Level 1** (with 4 trait points they may assign to their Attribute Triangle). With each level gained, they earn 1 new trait point they can assign.

The level of the character is denoted in the middle of the Attribute Triangle.

A level is gained upon earning the minimum amount of **experience points (Exp)** required to ascend (see *Level Advancement* below), which can be earned by felling enemies or completing certain challenges.

The maximum level in standard *Agon* games is 15 (totaling 18 trait points).

#### Fibonacci Experience Curve
The experience required per level-up follows the Fibonacci sequence through level 10, where the Exp for the next level equals the sum of the preceding two. After level 10 the climb levels off, so each level costs only a little more than the last.

Beyond Lv 3, you’ll want to push your character to try new, more difficult encounters in the world.

Strong enemies or difficult puzzles can award a greater amount of experience to compensate for the increasing gaps between levels. The Historian’s goal will be to equalize time-to-level as much as possible. Mileage will vary between games, though.

Players may record their Exp on their character sheets in the field beside the Attribute Triangle.

### Health Points (HP) and Death
Being partially mortal, a demi-god can still know death, but only temporarily at the hands of man. The slumber of true death can only come by the judgment and power of an **Olympian** (a true god). If killed by any other means, a demi-god will **dissipate** and later rise again at an altar to their parent. Additionally, as long as their covenant with their Aegis is still intact, *the familiar will enjoy the same benefits of immortality*.

Most temples to the gods outside of the walled cities have long since been destroyed; their altars were corrupted by the taint of monsters or abandoned altogether. Therefore, any likely place of resurrection will be within the nearest protected city or settlement. So long as a priest continues to bless an altar, a child of its patron god may awaken there.

In *Agon*, a character’s strength of vitality is determined by a pool of Hit Points (HP). Without modification from active or passive affinities, status effects, or traits, **the base Hit Points for all humanoid creatures**, including demi-gods, **is <u>30</u> (+ 2 per Level after Level 1)**. Thus, when creating your demi-god and Aegis for the first time, **assign them 30 HP to begin with**. These pools may change depending on which affinities they are granted by their parent and/or favoring god(s) and how many Trait points are assigned to **Endurance**.

![A dissipated demi-god rises again at a blessed altar](art/resurrection.svg)

:::note
##### Resolving for Damage
When taking damage from an attack, first subtract points from the damage roll that were mitigated by defenses (i.e. shields and elemental resistances). Whatever remains is then deducted from your current HP. **If your HP reaches 0, you will dissipate**. As described above, <u>dissipation is not death</u>. As a demi-god, you will always come back as long as your divine parent has a blessed altar for your body to return to.

##### Resurrection
If your character's HP reaches zero and they dissipate, they will revive at the closest altar to their parent (patron) deity. In order to keep the player engaged, this can occur instantly, though there is the option to delay the resurrection should the player wish to focus on controlling a surviving character for the time being (such as their Aegis, if the demi-god was slain and the Aegis survived).
:::

### Speed
The **Speed** box on the character sheet denotes how far (in meters) the character can move in a single turn in a combat encounter. This is considered a "normal" speed of movement and, given the nature of combat, is like a fast jog. You can choose to move up to your max Speed in distance, once, without consuming your entire turn. The base Speed for all humanoids is **9 meters** per turn, so put **9** in this box to start, then increase by **1 per Agility point**.

**Dashing** is a special action you can have your character take when it's their turn. When you Dash, you can move up to <u>double your Speed</u> in lineal meters, **but you will be unable to take any further action on your turn**. This is basically a mad sprint. It cannot be coupled with a normal movement.

#### Overheal
Some Affinities and items allow for extra HP to be temporarily added to your HP pool. Acquiring these points is considered **Overheal**, and the additional points are recorded in the box below the main HP box on your character sheet.

Overheal HP is subtracted first when taking damage, and cannot be returned through normal heals. There is no time or rest expiration on Overheal HP.

#### Speed Loss
Certain status effects from Affinities may adversely impact your character's normal Speed. Record the reduction of speed in the **Speed Loss** box of the character sheet (i.e. "-2"). Your character's *actual* Speed = *Speed - Speed Loss*.


### Agathós Daemons
In the **Agathós** box of your character sheet, you'll record the number of **Agathós Daemons** awarded to you. These are "good spirits" you may call upon to guarantee one Success outcome when desired, and are earned whenever you achieve a Critical Success on Trials. You may call upon a daemon when making any roll, spending 1 spirit per summon.

### Armor
You pick your armor at character creation: **light**, **medium**, **heavy**, or none at all. Describe it however you like; the rules only care about its class. See [Armor](#armor-classes) in *Equipment & Economy* for what each class does and what it costs.

### Your Spell List {#your-spell-list}
Every demi-god and Aegis knows the four fundamental spells. All others are learned from the [Spell List](#spell-list) in the appendices, which also explains how spells hit.

### Biography
The **Biography** page is a separate page of your character sheet that allows the player freedom to fill out their character's backstory. Some elements of a good bio to consider include: their place or home of origin, their occupation, their life before Sword Fall, their views of the gods and their mortal parent, and their politics (or lack thereof) in regards to the four city-states.

Other great notes you can here are details about their appearance aside from their weapons and armor. Do they have any scars (how did they get them)? Do they have heterochromia? How do they wear their hair?

Personality notes are also recommended! Are they reclusive and withdrawn from others, or are they the type to buy everyone a round at the bar?

Most crucially, what is their relationship like with their Aegis (or demi-god, if filling out the sheet for the Aegis)? The dynamic between your two characters should play out and evolve during the course of the story. So as they mature, you'll want to expand on this part.

The information you include on this page can help the Historian the most out of anything else, letting them weave an interactive story with *reasons* for your characters to get involved and *be active*.

# <span class="num">Part III</span> Divine Power {#divine-power}

Like their parents before them, the demi-gods that inhabit Altea can use Primordial magic for both offensive and defensive purposes. Notably, a demi-god’s power is hereditary, derived from their Olympian progenitor. And since each god and goddess is sovereign to a cosmological truth, their divine power can differ greatly from one another. A child of Zeus, for instance, could possess an ability to channel lightning; while a child of Tyche might display an Affinity for luck and fortune.

## Affinities {#affinities}
An **Affinity** is a demi-god’s connection to a Primordial or Conceptual magic. **Primordial magic**, known as *Goetia*, primarily encompasses raw elemental energies, such as fire, ice, lightning, and so on. Whereas, **Conceptual magic**, known as *Mageia*, encompasses ideals given form or function, such as deceit (which can give form to illusions), or fortune (which can bring constant good luck).

A demi-god’s possible Affinities are limited to either four all-together under their parent’s influence, or three under their parent’s plus one from another deity that favors them.

The Aegis familiars are exempt from this rule, however. Having been granted new life from Gaea, mother of creation, an Aegis has some choice in their Affinities, since they may choose which god to worship. Their patron deity then bequeaths them their respective Affinities. This can make an Aegis a well-balanced familiar that can cover for their master’s weaknesses, or support their master’s Affinities with greater potency.

Both demi-gods and Aegis familiars are limited to four Affinities each. Follow the rules outlined in [Character Creation](#affinities-on-the-sheet).

![A demi-god calls lightning and fire while a sword hangs above by a thread](art/divine-power.svg)

## Theion & the Limits of Magic {#theion-the-limits-of-magic}
Magical creatures, including familiars and demi-gods, weave powerful goetia and mageia using **Theion**: a divine energy that permeates Altea, and doubles not only as the impetus for Affinities, but also the veil that shrouds the universe's greatest secrets.

In *Agon* this mysterious energy is tracked using the **Theion Gauge**, which is a tool players will use to know when they can unleash their more powerful Affinities and spells that come with a time-limit to prevent spamming them.

There are four bands on the gauge: a Blue, Green, Yellow, and Red band. Some spells and Affinities are more powerful than others, warranting the need for higher levels of Theion required to use them.

### To Operate the Gauge

In *combat*, your demi-god and Aegis accumulate Theion. Every band starts **empty** and fills one level at the end of each round: Blue is full after **2** rounds, Green **3**, Yellow **4**, and Red **5**. A full band is ready to be let loose. Using an Affinity or spell that requires that band **empties** it, and it starts filling again at the end of the round, so Blue can be used every other round at most.

Players can use whatever method they wish to track their Theion on the gauge. One recommended method is to print the gauge out on paper, and use a coin or small figurine for each of the colored bands.

Virtual tabletops are a great way to track everyone's Theion at once: the interactive character sheet has a Theion gauge built in, and it works inside Owlbear Rodeo.

### Carryover Rules

Because some combat encounters can end up being short, players may not get to use their more powerful abilities all that often. To balance fun and unbridled destruction, the Historian can set different "carryover" rules for Theion, where Theion accumulated in the gauge during combat encounters does not disappear when combat ends, but can be *carried over* to the next encounter.

Below are two rulesets recommended for most *Agons*. The Historian may wish to devise their own rules, though, so be sure to clarify with them at the start of your campaign.

##### Red-Only Carryover
The Blue, Green, and Yellow bands are emptied when an encounter ends, but any Theion gathered in the Red band remains between encounters .

* The classic, tighter rule.

##### Carry (All or Color) Until Rest
The Historian will determine which colors, or if all bands, can be carried over between encounters, but the energy accumulated will be dispersed upon a **Full Rest** (see [Resting](#resting)).

* Considered the "standard" carryover rule (all bands).

:::note
##### Theion Lore
The different colors of Theion aren't just for game mechanics and preventing ability-spam. They're actually part of Altea's lore!

Red Theion is called *Photia*, yellow is *Líthos*, green is *Aéras*, and blue is *Húdōr*. Though they should not be correlated with the elements, they were named for Fire, Stone, Air, and Water, respectively, because of their vibrant colors when manifested as energy particles during experiments conducted by Pegemus scientists and a need to name the discoveries.

An actual clock-like device can be carried on a person to measure their accumulation of Theion energies. *This* is the Theion Gauge in-world.
:::

![The Theion Gauge](art/theion-gauge.svg)

## The Primordial Magics (Goetia) {#the-primordial-magics-goetia}
Primordial magic (goetia) is also known as "elemental" magic—a wild and raw power that is drawn from the foundations of the world. Each deity of the pantheon holds dominion over at least one element, and that dominion can be passed on to their children. If a demi-god holds dominion over an element, they can learn spells that align with it.

In *Agon*, four spells are the most basic, fundamental uses of goetia, and every demi-god and Aegis knows them: the cantrip *Spinthēr*, plus *Belos*, *Aspis*, and *Potamos*. ### Elemental Strengths & Weaknesses {#elemental-strengths-weaknesses}

Every element has both an advantage over and weakness against another. In combat, when elements compete, defenses like shields can only withstand half their **base HP** against the greater element. If, on the other hand, a defense has the superior element, then the *attacking* element's damage is halved (see [Weal and Woe](#weal-and-woe) for Trial scenarios outside combat).

When elements that have no counterplay with each other interact, their interaction is treated as "element neutral". Likewise, if an element is used against itself (i.e. water vs water), then the interaction is treated as "element neutral".

The **Light** and **Shadow** elements are outliers. Light has no weakness, but is still only strong against one element—Shadow; and Shadow is strong against all other elements *but* Light.

# <span class="num">Part IV</span> Equipment & Economy {#equipment-economy}

## Obol & Didra
Heroes still need to eat, reload, and patch their armor. This part of the book follows the money: what it is and what it's worth, what it buys (goods, weapons, and armor), and finally what you can make with your own hands.

### Obol
Obol is digital. Each city-state keeps its own **ledger**, and your account is your line in it. When an exchange buys your didra, the city creates new obol for you, so each city's obol is backed by the didra in its vaults.

With no network left, the four ledgers never connect: Antillosian obol only works where Antillos's towers reach. That's why you keep four accounts, and why 100 obol in one city isn't the same money as 100 in another.

### Didra and the Exchange
Exchanges buy didra at the city's rate and sell it back at a **25% markup**. Didra works everywhere, so carrying crystals or goods between cities is the only way to move wealth from one ledger to another.

:::note
##### Tip: Sell Where You Shop
Pegemus pays the most per crystal, but its prices are the highest too, so a crystal buys about the same anywhere. What changes is *what* each city sells cheaply. Sell your didra in the city where you plan to spend it.
:::

##### The Four Ledgers
| City | Pays per didra | Prices | Cheap here | Pricey here | Depth |
|:--|:--:|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|
| Antillos | 250o | ×1.0 | Services, lodging | – | 20 |
| Therone | 220o | ×0.9 | Food, alchemy, trade goods | Weapons | 15 |
| Tyríses | 300o | ×1.2 | Weapons, armor, ammo | Luxuries, alchemy | 25 |
| Pegemus | 400o | ×1.5 | Firearms, tech, refined materials | Food, lodging | 40 |

*Cheap* goods cost about 20% less than the city's prices, and *pricey* goods about 20% more. *Depth* is how much didra a city can absorb in a week (see *The Didra Market*). As a guide, a Standard monster's didra is worth about 375o, an Elite's about 1,100o, and a Legendary's about 2,750o.
### The Didra Market
Didra powers the lights, pumps, and relay towers that keep each ledger running, so a city that runs short of it feels it in its prices. Your Historian may track each city's market as it swings between Glut and Crisis (see *The Didra Market* in the [Historian's Handbook](@historian#the-didra-market)). If they don't, every city stays Steady.

## Weapons {#weapons}
The best defense is a good offense, as the old saying goes. Even demi-gods, with all their gifts and Affinities, need a trusty weapon or two at their side. For some, their Aegis is all that's required, but for others, a "backup plan" is a must.

Weapons come in six **types**: Melee, Thrown, Light Firearms, Heavy Firearms, Bows & Crossbows, and Shields. Every weapon in the tables is a specific make with its own stats, so a *Kestrel K-9* and a *Whisper P-12* are different pistols. Unique weapons not listed here can be discovered during play.

**Hit** rolls add your **Strength** points with melee weapons and shields, or your **Perception** points with bows, firearms, and thrown weapons; *Light* and *Finesse* weapons use **Agility** instead. **Damage** from melee and thrown weapons adds your Strength points (twice that with *Heavy* weapons). Bows and firearms add **half** your Perception points (rounded down) to damage; *Draw* adds your Strength instead, and *Deadeye* adds all of your Perception. See **Weapon Properties** for each property.

:::note
##### Reminder
Your Aegis starts knowing the **Aegis form** from its Starter Kit. An Aegis learns **one specific weapon** at a time: knowing the *Antillosian Gladius* doesn't teach it the *Tyrísian Longblade*. Learning a new one takes its **learning time (LT)**, in Full Rests. Simple weapons have no learning time, so your Aegis can learn them as soon as it finds one.
:::

### Magazines and “Loose” Components
**As a general rule**, an Aegis’ shapeshifting ability cannot account for components of a weapon that would too frequently separate from the main construct. This is why Aegis cannot include a firearm’s magazine and bullets when shifting (or arrows and a quiver to accompany a bow).

Although a modern gun is, in fact, comprised of many components, so too are swords (the blade, the hilt, the pommel, the grip, etc.). As long as these small components remain attached to one another, they are as limbs to the familiar; a skeleton held together by the cartilage of magic. They can move and shift, they can vibrate, or they can remain rigid; it matters not, so long as they are connected.

With all the above considered, it stands to reason that if an Aegis takes the shape of a gun, the master ought to have the correct magazines and ammunition required.
### Maximum Effective Range (MER)
All melee weapons have a 2m reach (range). If thrown, and *not* considered a throwing weapon, their maximum effective range is 8m, and they will deal half the Damage rolled (unless a Divine Strike), rounded down, with a minimum of 1 Dmg.

A **maximum effective range (MER)** is the range up to which point maximum damage will begin to fall off if exceeded. This is annotated in the weapon tables as simply “Range”.

At the Historian’s discretion, a player may attempt to attack a target beyond their weapon’s MER. If the hit is successful, and regardless of a Divine Strike, the damage rolled is reduced by 1pt for every 10m over the MER, with a minimum final damage of 1, and a minimum reduction by 1. The Historian may ask players to make a **Trial** to determine the success of exceeding a weapon’s MER.

### Weapon Properties
##### Handling
**Quick:** Make two attacks per Action, each with its own Hit and damage roll.

**Light:** Use **Agility** for Hit rolls. Light weapons add no trait to damage.

**Finesse:** Use **Agility** instead of Strength for both Hit and damage.

**Heavy:** Add **twice** your Strength points to damage. Needs both hands: no shield, and you can't hold a Concentration spell.

**One-Handed:** A bow or crossbow that needs only one hand.

**Reach:** Melee attacks reach 4m.

**Unarmed:** A fist, kick, or headbutt deals **1d4 + Strength** points, crit 20.

##### On the Hit
**Keen:** Widens the crit range by 2 (a Crit of 18 becomes 16).

**Brutal:** A Critical Hit or Divine Strike triples the damage dice instead of doubling them.

**Sunder:** Ignores shields (*Aspis*, *Protection*, and *Guard*).

**Cleave:** If the attack drops its target, make one more attack against another target within reach (once per Action).

**Ricochet:** If the attack drops its target, it strikes another target within 5m (roll a new Hit).

**Bleed:** On a Hit, the target is **Bleeding** (End).

**Shock:** On a Critical Hit or Divine Strike, the target is **Stunned** (End).

**Bind:** On a Hit, the target is **Bound** (Agi).

##### Shields
**Guard X:** While held, gain +X **Evasion**, and your **Parry** adds X more.

**Agile:** Your **Dodge** also adds the shield's Guard.

**Ballistic:** You can **Parry** ranged attacks with this shield.

**Cumbersome:** −3m Speed while you hold it.

##### Ranged
**Draw:** Add your **Strength** points to damage (instead of half Perception).

**Deadeye:** Add your **Perception** points to damage.

**Steady:** +2 to Hit rolls.

**Spread:** +2 to Hit within 10m. Beyond 20m, it deals half damage.

**Burst X:** One Hit roll and one damage roll per Action, spending X rounds; each damage die is a bullet. On a Graze, it deals half its damage instead.

**Blast X:** One Hit roll, compared to the Evasion of every creature within X m of the point you aim at. Single use, and an Aegis can't learn it.

**Suppressed:** Firing it doesn't give away your position.

**Concealed:** Easy to hide. A search misses it unless the searcher succeeds on a **Perception Trial** (TN 16).

**Flare:** On a Hit, the target is **Burning** (End). The flare lights everything within 30m for about a minute.

### Ready, Aim, Fire!
**Bows and crossbows** don't reload: nocking an arrow or cocking a bolt is narrative and costs nothing. Arrows and bolts are kept in a quiver.

**Firearms** hold the number of rounds in their **Mag** column. **Reloading costs a Minor Action**; the Cyclops M60's belt also costs **all of your movement**, since linking a new belt means getting down to do it.

## Armor {#armor-classes}
For the most part, *Agon* will lean on the “rule of cool” when it comes to a character’s appearance and what they are sporting for armor and clothing. Rather than list an exhaustive compendium of armors, cloaks, capes, and enchanted sneakers, armor will be divided into three weight classes: **light**, **medium**, and **heavy**.

You are free to choose between the three archetypal armors and describe what that armor looks like. The class you choose to wear is presumed to benefit the whole body and you cannot combine two different classes.

| Class | HP | Evasion | Also | Value |
|:--|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|
| Light | +5 | – | – | 500o |
| Medium | +15 | −1 | – | 1,000o |
| Heavy | +25 | −2 | **Woe** on Agility Trials | 2,000o |
| Unarmored | – | – | **Weal** on Agility Trials; **Rested Bonus** (+10% Exp until your next Full Rest) | – |

#### Light
Flexible leather padding, a second-chance vest, knee- or elbow-guards, and a light helmet. You can roll, vault, and sprint with almost no effort.

#### Medium
Sturdier vests, vambraces and leg guards, and visored helmets. You can still move with relative ease.

#### Heavy
Full-body suits of durable armor, weighted guards and pauldrons, and full face-covering helms. You can take a beating, but you can't move quickly or dodge fast attacks.

#### Unarmored
Just the clothes on your back, with no physical or magical protection. At least you'll sleep well.

:::note
Putting armor **on** or taking it **off** in combat takes a whole turn: your Action and your Movement.
:::

## Materials & Crafting
Every blade, round, and potion starts as raw material. ### Where Materials Come From
**Buy them** in the cities. Each city sells some families cheaply and others dearly (see *The Four Ledgers*): Pegemus is the place for Tech and Rare materials.

**Salvage them** from the ruins of the old world (see *Salvage*).

**Harvest them** from the monsters you kill (see *Monster Parts*). Harvesting a part takes a few minutes and a **Creativity Trial** (TN 12). On a Failure, the part is ruined.

**Refine them.** Each step up a family's ladder (Hide to Leather, Iron to Steel) is a refining job done during a Full Rest. *Crafting* covers the details.

##### Tip: Who Pays Best
Pegemus pays the most for Tech and Rare materials, Tyríses for metal and gunpowder, and Therone for alchemy ingredients and cloth. Sell your salvage where it's needed.

### Crafting {#crafting-rules}
Crafting happens during a **Full Rest**. On each Full Rest, every character can work **one crafting job** (an Aegis can too, unless it's busy learning a new form). A job is one of three things: **make an item**, **refine materials**, or **work a monster part** into gear you already own.

#### Materials
An item needs materials worth **half its value**, drawn from its *Craft* column, with at least one unit of each listed material. **Creativity** lowers that cost (see [Creativity](#creativity)).

#### Time
Bigger work takes more rests. Progress carries over, so you can set a project down and pick it up again later.

| Item value | Full Rests |
|:--|:--:|
| Up to 500o | 1 |
| Up to 2,000o | 2 |
| Up to 5,000o | 3 |
| Over 5,000o | 4 |

#### The Crafting Trial
When the job is done, make a **Creativity Trial** against a TN set by the item's value:

| Item value | TN |
|:--|:--:|
| Up to 500o | 8 |
| Up to 2,000o | 12 |
| Up to 5,000o | 16 |
| Over 5,000o | 20 |

A **Close Call** still succeeds. On a **Failure**, you lose only time: keep your materials and finish the job on your next Full Rest. On a **natural 20**, you've made a **masterwork**, worth 25% more.

#### Schematics
Simple items (those with "–" in the *LT* column), ammunition, and basic gear need no schematic. Everything else does. Buy one in a city that sells that kind of gear for **25% of the item's value**, or find one in the ruins. A schematic is yours forever. Items marked **\* Affinity** can only be made by a crafter with the **Craftsmanship** Affinity, who needs no schematics at all.

#### Tools and Workshops
Every job needs the right kit (see *Goods & Services*): **smith's tools** for blades, armor, shields, and arrows; **gunsmith's tools** for firearms and ammo; an **alchemy set** for Nectar, Panacea, smoke bombs, and gunpowder; a **tinker's kit** for Tech. Items worth more than **2,000o** also need a proper **workshop**, rented in a city by the day.

#### Refining
Refining moves materials one step up a family's ladder: **2 units become 1 unit** of the next step (Hide to Leather is 1 for 1). One job refines up to **5 units**, or just **1** of a Rare material. Polymer, High Polymer, and Tech can't be refined by hand: buy them or salvage them.

#### Working Monster Parts
Add a monster part while crafting a new item, as part of the same job, or work it into gear you already own as its own job: **1 Full Rest** and a **Creativity Trial (TN 16)**. On a Failure, the part survives and you can try again next rest. Each item holds **one** part.

#### Selling Your Work
* **Loot and used gear** sell for **half** their value.
* **Your own craftwork** sells for **75%** of its value. You made it, and buyers trust it.
* **Craftsmanship** makes your work worth 25% more, on top of that.
* **Commissions** pay **full value or better**: an NPC orders a piece, and the Historian sets the deadline and the bonus.


:::note
##### Example: Forging a Gladius
Say your demi-god has **Creativity 2** (10% off) and the schematic for an **Antillosian Gladius** (300o). Its materials should be worth half its value, 150o, less 10%: **about 135o** of Steel and Leather, so 1 Steel and 2 Leather. It's under 500o, so the job takes **1 Full Rest** and a **TN 8** Creativity Trial. A +4 modifier makes that nearly certain. Wield it, or sell it for 225o.
:::

## Spell List {#spell-list}

### Casting Spells
**Every damaging spell rolls to Hit.** Heals, shields, walls, and boons don't. Fundamentals need only the Blue band (Spinthēr needs none).

Spells roll to Hit like a weapon: **d20 + your Intuition points** against the target's Evasion, with the same outcomes (spells crit only on a natural 20).

**Graze:** when a spell misses by 1-4, the target still takes damage equal to your **Power** modifier (Spinthēr and Xiphē: your Power pts). No curses apply.

**"+ Power"** means your Power modifier (points × 2). **"+ Power pts"** means your Power points.

**Areas:** roll one Hit and compare it to each creature in the area. Areas hit friend and foe alike unless the spell says otherwise.

**Curses** (see *Boons and Curses*) land on a Hit (not a Graze) only if the Hit total also meets the target's Resistance (10 + its points in the curse's trait).

## Boons and Curses {#boons-and-curses}
**Boons** help and **curses** hinder. Spells, Affinities, and some weapons apply them.

**Landing a curse:** a curse rides on a hit. If the attack Hits (a Graze isn't enough), compare the same Hit total to the target's **Resistance: 10 + its points in the curse's trait**. Meet or beat it, and the curse lands. Curses from sources with no Hit roll land unless the source says otherwise.

**Boons** land automatically on a willing creature.

**Duration:** until the end of the affected creature's next turn, unless the table or the source says otherwise. **Stacking:** the same boon or curse doesn't stack; applying it again resets its duration and keeps the stronger value. **Removing:** anything that cures or dispels (the Healing Affinity, Law's *Dispel*, *Pēgē*) ends one curse.

## Items {#items}
What each item does. Prices are in [Goods & Services](#goods-services). Unless an entry says otherwise, an item is used up when you use it, and using it in combat takes a **Minor Action**, on yourself or on someone within reach.

#### Medicine
**Medical Kit.**
* *In combat:* the patient regains **1d6 + 2** HP, and **Bleeding** ends.
* *Out of combat:* with about 10 minutes of proper care, the patient regains **2d6 + 2** HP instead.
* *On a rest:* one kit lets one character recover HP on a Short or Full Rest (see *Resting*).

**Nectar.** A thinned draught of the gods' own drink, distilled by alchemists. The drinker regains **2d6 + 4** HP. You can pour it down the throat of an ally who's down.

**Panacea Injector.** Named for the goddess of cure-alls. It ends **every curse** on the target, even *Doomed*.

:::note
##### Tip: The Healing Affinity
A character with the **Healing** Affinity adds **1d6 + Power** to the HP restored by a Medical Kit or Nectar.
:::

#### Alchemy & Tricks
**Smoke Bomb.** Throwing it is an **Action**, up to 10 m. Smoke fills a **5 m radius** until the end of your next turn. Attacks into, out of, or through the cloud are made as if **Blinded** (no trait bonus to Hit), with no Resistance.

**Draught of Proteus.** Named for the shapeshifting sea god. For about an hour, you take the **appearance and voice** of someone you've seen, but not their memories, abilities, or Affinities. Taking damage ends it, and anyone with **Order** or **Cunning** sees through it.

#### Tools & Traps
**Snare Trap.** Setting one takes about a minute, so not in combat. The first creature to step in is **Bound** (no Resistance) until it spends an Action to break free. It can't hold anything much bigger than a horse, or any Legendary. Spotting one takes a **Perception Trial** (TN 12). A sprung snare can be reset.

**Bait Bag.** Beasts and Frenzied within about 50 m who catch the scent come to investigate. One bag, one lure.

**Lockpick Set.** You need one to pick a lock: an **Agility Trial**, TN 12 for a simple lock, 16 for a secure one, 20 for a vault. It isn't used up.

**Rope & Grappling Hook.** Catching a ledge is a **Perception Trial** (TN 12). Once it holds, climbing takes no roll unless someone's shooting at you. Neither is used up.

#### Camping Gear
**Tent and Blankets.** Shelter for **Rough Camping** (see *Resting*): in harsh weather, a Full Rest without shelter counts only as a Short Rest.

**Cook Set, Canteen, Rations, Shovel.** No special rules, but the Historian may ask what you're eating, drinking, and digging with.

#### Crafting Gear
**Alembic, Potion Vials, Ingredient Satchel.** Tools for brewing; see [Crafting](#crafting-rules).

:::note
##### Tip: Out Past the Towers
Medicine and Nectar make excellent trade goods where obol doesn't work. A frontier settlement may trade a night's lodging and a guide for a single kit.
:::
